Ground-Zero Communication Blackout · Post-Disaster Information Fog

Zone — a phone network that survives when everything else is gone

Towers down, no signal, no internet. Phones talk straight to each other over Bluetooth. A trapped person's phone broadcasts a 31-byte distress packet; any phone in range copies it and carries it. The signal walks out of the disaster zone in someone's pocket.

Collection with no network. Fusion with no ground truth. Same broken wire, two ends.

One app, three roles

Citizen

The trapped or stranded person. No menus, no login — it broadcasts even if the phone is buried or they're unconscious. Shows one line: “3 devices are carrying your signal.”

Responder

The rescuer at the edge. A triage-ranked list of who's been heard, and a Dig Here proximity bar — closer to a metal detector than a map.

Map / EOC

The control room. A severity grid showing which areas are hit how hard — and which ones went completely dark, with the minute it happened.

Why nobody else has this

Silence is the signal. Every packet declares when the device will next transmit. So when a device goes quiet, the system knows whether that silence was expected — a dying battery — or not. A phone at 70% that promised to speak in ten seconds and vanished at three: something happened to it, and we know within one interval when.

Bridgefy, Briar, goTenna are messaging apps. Chat has no concept of an expected next message, so silence there means nothing. This works because it's structured telemetry, not messaging — the differentiator and the architecture are the same decision.

Known limitations — stated, not hidden

  • iOS can't participate. Apple restricts BLE advertising; Web Bluetooth gives browsers the central role only. A real deployment needs an entitlement or a hardware beacon. Android only.
  • 2.4 GHz penetrates rubble badly. The answer isn't magic penetration — a pinned phone advertises for days at low duty cycle, and a rescuer walking the pile eventually gets within ~10 m. We turn “search this block” into “search this circle.”
  • Coded-PHY support is chipset-dependent. It doesn't track price or release year, so the radio runs dual-mode and falls back to 1M where extended advertising is missing.
  • The last mile is out of scope. The responder's phone is the gateway. Even if it never syncs, “47 signals in this building, 3 marked rising-water” is already the whole product.
