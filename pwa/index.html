Post-disaster communication blackout

The network is gone. Phones still talk.

When towers fall and the internet dies, Zone phones pass a tiny distress signal from hand to hand over Bluetooth, until it walks out of the disaster zone in someone's pocket.

And when a whole cluster of phones goes quiet at once, Zone tells you exactly when, and where.

The problems it takes off the table

Trapped people can’t call for help. No signal.
Their phone reports itself, hands-free.
Rescuers don’t know where survivors are.
A live list builds at the perimeter.
Can’t tell a roof from a basement.
The barometer gives height to ±1–2 m, where GPS is dead.
Reports are fragmented and contradictory.
One panicking phone is a ghost; three that agree are a signal.
Phones die in hours.
Battery-aware: days of honest heartbeats.
The one nobody else has
Can’t tell a flattened village from a flooded one.
CELL_LOSS: a cluster that goes silent together means the area was destroyed, at a known minute.
Why nobody else has this
Chat apps can’t notice silence. Zone is built on it.

Every packet declares when the device will next transmit. A phone at 70% battery that promised to speak in ten seconds and vanished at three has had something happen to it, and we know within one interval when. A dying battery is expected. This is not.

Bridgefy, Briar and goTenna are messaging apps. Messaging has no idea of an expected next message. Zone is structured telemetry, so the differentiator and the architecture are the same decision.

Known limits

Stated, not hidden

iPhone can’t join the mesh

Apple restricts Bluetooth advertising, and browsers can only listen. A real deployment needs an entitlement or a cheap hardware beacon. Today it is Android only. The control-room view works anywhere.

Bluetooth doesn’t pass through rubble well

We don’t pretend otherwise. A pinned phone keeps advertising for days at low power, and a rescuer walking the pile eventually gets within about 10 m. That turns “search this block” into “search this circle.”

Long-range mode depends on the phone’s chip

Coded-PHY support doesn’t track price or release year. So the radio runs dual-mode and falls back to standard Bluetooth where long range is missing.

The last mile is out of scope

The responder’s phone is the gateway. Even if it never syncs, “47 signals in this building, 3 marked rising water” is already the whole product.
