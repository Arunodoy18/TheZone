Offline disaster signal network · field explainer

The Dead Man's Packet

Zone is a phone app that keeps working when every network is gone. Phones talk straight to each other over Bluetooth, and a trapped person's signal physically walks out of the disaster zone in a rescuer's pocket. Its one original idea: every packet says when the phone will speak next — so when a phone goes quiet, the network can tell whether that silence was expected or whether the phone was destroyed.

01The problem

In the first 72 hours after an earthquake, two failures kill people who were survivable:

  • Communication blackout. Cell towers are down or overloaded. A person pinned under a slab has a charged phone in their pocket and no way to use it. Rescuers on the pile have no way to hear them.
  • Information fog. The command post cannot tell total structural collapse from minor waterlogging, or “search this whole block” from “search this ten-metre circle.” Crews get sent to buildings that are already clear while calls go unanswered a street away.

Existing mesh-messenger apps (Bridgefy and the like) carry chat between people who are both fine and both holding their phones. They do nothing for someone unconscious, and they treat a phone going offline as a non-event — exactly the event that matters here.

02What Zone is

One Android APK. Native Kotlin, no servers, no internet — it runs identically in airplane mode. It has three modes, chosen once on first launch:

Citizen

The trapped person

Opens straight into broadcasting. Dark screen to save battery and not give away position. One line — “You are being heard” — and one number: how many phones have carried your signal. Three optional buttons: Trapped / Water rising / Safe.

Responder

The rescuer on the pile

Built for gloves, rain and sunlight. A live list of every heard person, sorted by triage priority, not by time. Tap a row for Dig Here — a proximity bar that fills as you walk toward the signal. A metal detector, not a map.

Map / EOC

The command post

A grid-cell severity map for the projector. Cells are coloured by confidence-scored severity. A cell where every phone went silent at once gets its own visual language and a timestamp. A time scrubber replays the whole event.

03How a signal leaves the zone

No phone-to-phone pairing, no connection. Every phone continuously broadcasts a tiny packet in its Bluetooth advertisement, and every phone listens. The relay is three moves:

1
Broadcast.

The trapped phone advertises a 31-byte packet — who, where, status, altitude, battery, and when it will transmit again. It repeats on a schedule set by its battery level.

2
Carry.

Any phone in range copies the packet into its store and starts rebroadcasting it with the hop count bumped by one. The original is never modified. Duplicates are dropped by content hash, so the same message never loops.

3
Deliver.

That second phone is walked — by a person, in a pocket — out to a responder or the command post, which now holds the trapped person’s packet at hop count 2. The signal crossed the gap on foot.

Merging two phones’ stores is just set-union over hash-addressed records — a conflict-free merge (a CRDT) by construction, with no library.

04The USP — the Dead Man's Packet

Every packet carries one field the rest of the system is built around: next_expected_tx — the number of seconds until the sender intends to transmit again. It is computed honestly from the sender’s current battery rung, never faked.

For every phone it has heard, a receiver tracks last_heard_at, the promised interval, and last known battery. On a timer it classifies each one:

ConditionVerdictMeaning
overdue < graceALIVEA missed advert or two. Normal for Bluetooth.
battery ≤ criticalEXPECTED SILENCEIt warned us it was going quiet. Deprioritise, don’t alarm.
overdue > 3× promisedUNEXPECTED SILENCEIt promised to speak in 60 s and vanished at 12 with 70% battery. The phone was destroyed. Escalate.

Then the cell-level rule, which is the whole point on the map:

If a ~100 m grid cell holds ≥ 3 phones and ≥ 80% of them enter unexpected silence inside the same 120-second window → CELL LOSS. The area collapsed.

Every state change is written to an in-app log with a wall-clock timestamp, so the demo can point at the exact second a phone went dark. This also produces a triage signal no other system has: you can rank victims by who is about to stop transmitting — reach the phone at 4% before it goes silent, not after.

05Silence is the signal

The single most important visual decision in the project: a cell where everyone went quiet at once must never look like a cell that never had coverage. If those two read the same, the idea is invisible on stage.

06The 31-byte packet

Bluetooth’s legacy advertising payload is 31 bytes. Design for more and you maintain two encoders under pressure, so the core is exactly 31 — “a life in 31 bytes.” Big-endian, carried in the manufacturer-data field.

version / type device id (6 B) position delta (4 B) status · severity / casualties timestamp battery / hops next_expected_tx — the USP altitude delta + trend auth (4 B) reserved (7 B)
OffFieldWhat it carries
0version_typeProtocol version + packet type (0 = status).
1–6device_idFirst 6 bytes of a SHA-256 hash of a per-install random key. The whole identity story — no crypto library.
7–10positionLat/lon delta from a local origin, ~1–2 m precision. A reserved value means “no GPS fix” — position is then inferred from the relay path.
11statusUnknown / Safe / Trapped / Rising water / Injured / Has resource / Responder.
12severity_casualtiesSeverity 0–15 and casualty count 0–15 packed into one byte.
13–14timestampMinutes since a fixed event epoch.
15battery_hopsBattery in 16 steps + hop count 0–15, one byte.
16–17next_expected_txSeconds until the next transmission. The field the whole USP runs on. Must stay honest at every battery level.
18alt_deltaRelative altitude in metres vs this phone’s launch baseline. A reserved value means “no barometer,” never a false zero.
19–22authFirst 4 bytes of SHA-256(key ‖ payload). Anti-spoofing at demo scale, not real signatures.
23alt_trendMetres of altitude change over the last 3 transmissions. Positive = climbing.
24–30reservedZero-filled. Room for a future text-code index.

Content identity hashes over the message bytes but excludes the hop nibble and auth, so a message is one record no matter how many times it is relayed.

07Every feature in the build

Transport

Connectionless BLE
Broadcast-only advertising in the manufacturer-data field. No GATT, no pairing — far more robust than a connection in rubble.
Dual PHY
Advertises and scans on both 1M and Coded PHY (S=8, eight transmissions per bit for range). Falls back to legacy 1M on chipsets without extended advertising, so the network never silently partitions by handset.
Foreground service
A persistent notification (“You are being heard”) keeps whatever transport is active alive across screen lock — Android kills a background advertiser otherwise.
Transport is an interface
BLE, an in-process 500-node simulator, and a JSON file import/export all implement the same contract. Every piece of logic runs identically on any of them — if Bluetooth fails at 3am, the demo still runs on the simulator.

Packet & store

31-byte codec
Pure Kotlin, zero Android dependencies, covered by unit tests including a deliberate test that two packets differing only in hop count still hash as one message.
Content-addressed store
Dedup by hash; the same signal heard from five phones is stored once. Merge is set-union — conflict-free by design.
Relay without mutation
Rebroadcasts a hop-incremented copy, round-robin across carried packets so relaying never starves the phone’s own signal. Drops at hop 15.

Dead Man's Packet

Per-device silence
ALIVE / OVERDUE / EXPECTED / UNEXPECTED classification on every tick, with a generous grace period tuned on real phones.
Cell-loss detection
The ≥3-phones, ≥80%, 120-second rule over ~100 m grid cells — the collapse signal.
Timestamped transition log
Every state change written to an in-app log with wall-clock time, so the exact moment of going dark is on screen.

Triage

Single-score priority
The responder list sorts by a computed rank, not recency: rising water + climbing altitude first, then just-lost (unexpected silence under 5 min), then trapped below grade, then anyone about to go silent on low battery, then severity.
Reason line
Each row states why it is ranked where it is — “rising water, climbing.”

Barometer

Relative altitude
Pressure sensor → metres above or below a baseline captured at launch. Works indoors where GPS is dead. Absolute altitude is discarded — only the delta matters.
Trend & auto-escalation
Altitude change over the last 3 transmissions. A rising trend under “Rising water” escalates to drowning priority with no user input.
Graceful absence
Phones without a barometer are flagged as such, never reported as zero metres.

Battery-adaptive duty cycle

Interval + PHY ladder
>60% → 1 s on Coded PHY. 30–60% → 10 s alternating. 10–30% → 60 s on 1M. <10% → 300 s, sparse — but still broadcasting an accurate next_expected_tx.
Honest at every level
A phone at 3% that keeps truthfully declaring a 300-second interval is still fully part of the network. A phone that lies here breaks the whole system.

Confidence & proximity

Corroboration scoring
Map-cell severity is weighted by how many distinct phones reported it, how many independent relay paths it arrived on, whether the physics is plausible, and whether a verified responder confirmed it. Low-confidence cells render ghosted.
Sybil resistance
Only physically-plausible reports feed the device count, so a flood of packets from fabricated ids can’t inflate a cell. A bare “responder” byte earns nothing — trust comes only from a responder packet that verifies against the shared key.
Near-damage alert
A phone that finds itself one hop from a collapsed cell tightens its own beacon interval — the network densifies exactly where it matters.

Three interfaces

Citizen
One headline, one number, three buttons. The count springs and the phone buzzes the instant another phone picks up the signal.
Responder
High-contrast daylight list; rows slide to their new rank as triage changes. Dig Here proximity bar with a link-quality readout and a haptic tick at each range band.
Map
Severity grid, distinct silence rendering, coverage-gap hints, a “send here first” priority strip, and a replay scrubber.

Demo & scale

Seeded simulator
500 synthetic nodes over Rasuwagadhi geography, replaying the real toll curve (22 → 95 → 469 → 626 over 72 hours) compressed to about 90 seconds, with a scripted mass-silence event so cell loss fires on cue.
Browser EOC viewer
A zero-dependency PWA that draws the severity map and triage list from a snapshot. Opened as ?live=eoc.json it polls a same-origin file every 3 s — the phone rewrites that snapshot every 4 s, so a laptop at the command post tracks the field with no network.
Hidden battery override
A long-press gesture cycles a faked battery level so the duty-cycle ladder can be shown on stage on fully charged phones.

Since the hackathon build (v0.3)

Crash-safe state
The store, the confirmed collapses and the silence tracks are written to disk and reloaded on start, so a reboot or an OS kill mid-incident doesn’t wipe a carrier phone. Atomic write, 24 h age cutoff.
Real position
The heartbeat sends an actual GPS fix (last-known, no Play Services, no network) as a delta from a configurable per-incident origin — falling back to no-fix, where the relay path localises the device.
Inferred TRAPPED
A phone held dead still for five minutes, with no user input, broadcasts TRAPPED_DEBRIS — the “works when unconscious” claim, made true.
Pre-shared responder key
A responder phone provisioned with a shared secret signs its packets with a SHA-256 MAC any phone can verify (no crypto library). A forged “responder” is rejected; a real one lifts a cell’s confidence.
RESOLVE
A responder marks a signal reached — a signed type-1 packet carrying the target’s content-id. It rides the same store-carry-forward, and every phone and the EOC drop that report, so the picture converges on who still needs help.
Hot / cold Dig Here
A least-squares fit over the RSSI slope while the responder walks — WARMER / HOLDING / COLDER — so the last few steps read as toward the signal or away from it.
Headcount
An optional “people with you” stepper on the Citizen screen feeds the packet’s casualty count.
Sneakernet
Export the whole mesh state to a JSON file, share it by any means the phone has, import and set-union merge it on another phone — the fallback when BLE is dead and there is no cable.
Language
The Citizen screen — the one a local victim reads — in English, Hindi and Nepali, picked on the landing screen.
Field readiness
Real app identity and adaptive icon; an on-device crash log (no analytics, no network); a “Keep Zone alive” screen that walks the Doze exemption and per-OEM autostart settings; CI running the unit tests on every push.

08What we deliberately left out

Stated up front, because a limitation you declare is respected and one that is discovered is not.

CutWhy it is defensible
iOS devicesiOS Safari has no Web Bluetooth at all, and native iOS background advertising is locked down by Apple. Two iOS phones would both be listeners and never hear each other. A real deployment needs an Apple entitlement or a hardware beacon.
Per-device signaturesThe RESPONDER tier now verifies against a pre-shared key (a SHA-256 MAC — no library). Full per-device Ed25519 identity is still cut: it needs a crypto dependency and more than 31 bytes.
RSSI trilaterationA proximity bar plus a hot/cold trend converts “this block” into “this 10 m circle.” True trilateration needs movement tracking that would not have been finished.
A CRDT libraryMerging hash-addressed records by union already is a CRDT. Importing one would add weight and nothing else.
An internet gatewayOut of scope by design. The responder’s own phone showing “47 signals in this building, 3 rising-water” is already the whole product. A continuous local snapshot and a sneakernet file cover the command-post hop; a bearer to the wider world is future work.
Rubble penetration claims2.4 GHz attenuates hard through concrete and rebar. The answer is not magic range — it is that a pinned phone advertises for days at low power, and a rescuer walking the pile eventually gets within 10 m.
