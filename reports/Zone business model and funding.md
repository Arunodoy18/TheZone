# Zone needs ₹1.5 lakh now, a buyer later

Zone's realistic near-term path is not a funding round — it is a **₹1.0–1.5 lakh (~US$1,050–1,600) bridge budget** spent on device-lab phones, a Play Console fee, and local field-test logistics, funded through no-incorporation-required channels (hackathon prizes, SMIT's own pre-incubation resources, and a direct outreach to IIM Calcutta Innovation Park, which is now both the Northeast's regional entrepreneurship-fund administrator and Sikkim's own state-appointed incubation partner). The eventual business is almost certainly **B2G licensing to a state disaster management authority or NDMA-adjacent body**, not a consumer app monetized directly — every comparable product in this space (Bridgefy, goTenna, Everbridge) that survived did so by selling to institutions, not citizens, and the ones that stayed consumer-only and grant-funded (Briar, FireChat) are either marginal or defunct. The competitive landscape is genuinely favorable: no funded Indian competitor exists, Zone's core "silence-as-signal" dead man's switch mechanism has no deployed precedent in any disaster-communication product researched, and the regulatory path is clear — Bluetooth operates in license-exempt spectrum, and the app's zero-backend architecture avoids data-protection exposure entirely. But the team must stay honest about sequencing: DPIIT-gated instruments (SISFS, AIC-SMUTBI's seed fund) and India's largest disaster-tech government contracts both sit on the far side of incorporation and multi-year vendor-eligibility floors the team cannot presently clear, so the next 6–12 months should be spent on proof, not fundraising.

## Nobody else has built the dead man's switch for people

The single most defensible claim in Zone's whole pitch is architectural, not commercial: across every consumer and commercial disaster-communication product examined — Bridgefy, Briar, goTenna, FireChat, Zello — **none has a field or mechanism analogous to `next_expected_tx`**, the declared-heartbeat primitive that lets Zone's network mathematically distinguish a device that silently vanished from one that scheduled a long gap. Bridgefy is architecturally a store-and-forward chat/broadcast layer with no delivery confirmation at all; two independent cryptographic audits (2020's "Breaking Bridgefy" and a 2022 USENIX Security follow-up after Bridgefy adopted Signal's libsignal protocol) found it still failed its own security claims, transmitted user IDs in plaintext, and could be disabled network-wide by a single crafted "zip bomb" message ([USENIX 2022](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity22/presentation/albrecht); [IACR 2021/214](https://ia.cr/2021/214)). Briar requires manual, in-person-verified pairwise trust before any message moves at all — the opposite of Zone's design goal of a stranger's phone relaying a packet with zero pairing step ([Briar overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Briar_(software))). goTenna solves range by selling a **$849 external LoRa/VHF radio** that must physically pair to each phone, which caps the network to however many radios a buyer purchased — fundamentally incompatible with Zone's "every phone already has the radio it needs" model ([Armada International](https://www.armadainternational.com/2019/09/this-small-mesh-networking-device-from-gotenna-could-unblock-signal-denied-warfare-environments/)). FireChat is confirmed dead (official site 404, no updates since 2018), and Zello has zero offline mesh capability — its Bluetooth support exists only to pair headset accessories ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/us/zello-app-guide,news-25805.html)). The closest the literature gets to Zone's silence-detection logic is 2024 academic IoT fault-detection research ("Disaster-FD") and Cisco-linked patents on sensor-node liveness in low-power networks — both infrastructure-monitoring concepts, never applied to a person's survival status or aggregated spatially across a cluster of nearby strangers' phones to infer a structural-collapse event ([SBC/SOL paper](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/wtf/article/view/29768); [Google Patents US10075360B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10075360)). No Indian competitor exists either: the only domestic analog found is a 2025 Smart India Hackathon student prototype (Team ARCNET, first prize, BLE-based disaster mesh) with no funding, no company, and no fielded product — a signal that the problem is recognized but genuinely unclaimed ground ([Vidya Academy](https://news.vidyaacademy.ac.in/?p=76352)).

## Zone complements, not competes with, every adjacent system

Zone's go-to-market story benefits from a layered-stack framing rather than a head-to-head competitive one, because every system with real institutional weight sits at a different point in the disaster timeline or depends on infrastructure that fails exactly when Zone activates. NDMA's **SACHET** platform — built and owned by C-DOT (a Department of Telecom R&D body), integrating IMD, CWC, INCOIS, and GSI as alert-generating agencies — delivers location-based SMS, cell broadcast, and app push, but every one of those channels requires a functioning mobile network; the moment towers go down in a collapse, SACHET has no way to reach anyone and no way to learn anything about who's still alive ([GSMA case study](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-for-development/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Indias-SACHET-Public-Warning-System.pdf)). That is precisely the gap Zone fills: SACHET is government-to-citizen hazard warning *before* infrastructure fails; Zone is citizen-to-citizen status telemetry *after* it has. Starlink's 2026 disaster deployments (Colombia's August earthquake, the Philippines' Mindanao quake, Venezuela) restored regional internet backhaul within days by flying in dishes and partnering with telcos — useful for coordination and logistics, but it does nothing for a person trapped under rubble in the first hours and carries no person-level liveness concept at all ([driveteslacanada.ca](https://driveteslacanada.ca/spacex/starlink-free-internet-colombia-earthquake/); [Globe newsroom](https://www.globe.com.ph/about-us/newsroom/corporate/globe-deploys-starlink-satellite-tech-in-mindanao)). SACHET itself has no documented empanelment process for private technology vendors — it is a closed government-to-government architecture — so Zone's realistic positioning to NDMA-adjacent stakeholders is "the last-mile offline layer that keeps working exactly where SACHET's channels go dark," not "integration partner," since no evidence supports the latter being achievable.

## Institutions pay for mesh tech; citizens mostly don't

Every comparable product's revenue history points the same direction: sustainable money in this category comes from governments and institutions, not consumer payments. **Everbridge**, a NASDAQ-listed "critical event management" SaaS company, is the strongest template — it sells per-seat, per-feature annual-in-advance contracts running 1–3 years, grew revenue from $76.8M (2016) to $147.1M (2018), and already has named deployments with Kerala's, Odisha's, and Andhra Pradesh's disaster-management authorities, per the company's own case-study material ([Everbridge 10-K FY2018, SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1437352/000156459019005821/evbg-10k_20181231.htm)). goTenna's trajectory tells the same story from the hardware side: it moved from consumer mesh radios toward military and public-safety buyers, eventually holding a $15M Air Force contract and a $99M IDIQ with U.S. Customs and Border Protection before being acquired by a defense company ([Tectonic Defense](https://www.tectonicdefense.com/exclusive-forterra-acquires-gotenna/)). Bridgefy is the closest stage-appropriate precedent for Zone specifically: it started as a free consumer offline-messaging app, proved consumer monetization alone couldn't sustain the company, and pivoted to licensing its SDK to enterprises and governments — its CEO disclosed a $6M, four-year contract with one of the world's 15 largest cities, plus paid pilots with Ford and the UN ([The Pitch podcast](https://www.thepitch.show/144-bridgefy)). By contrast, the two mesh apps with no disclosed revenue model — Briar (sustained purely by a rotating set of digital-rights grants: Open Technology Fund, Internews, the Prototype Fund) and FireChat (free, no monetization found, now defunct) — are also the smallest and most precarious, a pattern that should discourage the team from treating grants as a long-term sustainability plan rather than a bridge. India's own procurement reality sets real expectations here too: the clearest named examples of government-to-vendor disaster-tech contracts (Everbridge for Kerala, Previstar for Gujarat, RIMES for Odisha under a World Bank program) all went to established vendors or academic MoU partners, and a real NDRF tender on the Government e-Marketplace required three years' prior experience and ₹50 lakh minimum turnover — a floor that excludes any pre-registration team today ([NDRF GeM bid](https://www.ndrf.gov.in/sites/default/files/GeM%20Bid%20no%206068815%20and%20Corrigendium%20for%20Aquatic%20Disaster%20Response%20Course-ADRC.pdf)). The actionable reading is not "go sell to NDMA now" — it is "build the pilot evidence that makes Zone a credible sub-vendor or technology partner to an academic/incubator-held MoU later," the same channel OSDMA used to bring in Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham for a landslide warning system.

## The next ₹1–1.5 lakh buys proof, not a product

The team's actual cash need over the next 6–12 months is modest and almost entirely about **de-risking hardware assumptions empirically**, because spec sheets cannot be trusted for the two BLE capabilities Zone depends on. Coded PHY support and barometer presence both vary by chipset and even by regional SKU in ways spec pages get wrong — a Nordic Semiconductor forum thread found a Xiaomi Pocophone F1 advertised long-range Bluetooth support on paper but failed to detect a Coded PHY device in practice, while a Galaxy S10 worked and an iPhone XS/11 did not ([Nordic DevZone](https://test-devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/33060/is-there-any-smartphone-with-supporting-of-bluetooth-5-long-range-feature/229036)), and GSMArena's own sensor listings mark the Galaxy A54's barometer "USA only," meaning the Indian retail unit cannot be assumed to match ([GSMArena](https://m.gsmarena.com/samsung_galaxy_a54-12070.php)). No single ₹15,000–30,000 phone was confirmed anywhere in this research to have both Coded PHY and a barometer, which is exactly why CLAUDE.md's H0 capability-probe step is mandated first in the build order — it is the only reliable source of truth, and the budget has to fund buying hardware provisionally, testing it, and returning what fails. A realistic device lab (four incremental phones beyond the team's existing 2–3 — one Snapdragon, one MediaTek Dimensity, one Samsung A5x-class for barometer odds, one legacy/no-Coded-PHY control to validate the fallback path) runs **₹75,000–95,000**, with a further **₹0–20,000** contingency if the first barometer candidate's Indian SKU comes up empty. Layered on top: a one-time, Verified **₹2,375 (US$25)** Google Play Console registration fee with no recurring cost ([Google Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/113468)); **₹16,000–21,000** for 2–3 nRF52840 boards to prototype a pinned hardware beacon (domestic IndiaMART/Amazon.in sourcing avoids import-duty uncertainty on the US-distributor sticker price); and **₹9,500–14,000** across three Gangtok-area day trips with a hired vehicle for range calibration and the H8 three-phone validation step, since no specific Gangtok–Rangpo fare exists in public sources and this needs a direct local quote. Pre-incorporation compliance costs **₹0** for now: Bluetooth's 2.4 GHz band is confirmed license-exempt in India, the team's own phones and off-the-shelf beacon boards fall under a 2019 DoT test/prototype exemption from WPC Equipment Type Approval, and Zone's zero-backend, no-personal-data-leaves-the-device architecture per CLAUDE.md does not create a data-fiduciary relationship under India's DPDP Act — though all three of these are this research's reasoned inference from public regulatory text, not legal advice, and a ₹5,000–15,000 lawyer consultation should be budgeted the moment any pilot involves real bystanders' phones, not before. Total: **≈₹1.0–1.5 lakh (US$1,050–1,600)**, with the device lab as the widest and most important swing to fund generously, since a failed phone purchase costs more in lost build-order time than it does in rupees.

## Where the money comes from without incorporating

Every scheme in this research that doesn't require DPIIT recognition or incorporation turns out to be either closed, hard-to-qualify, or a single clear lead worth chasing directly. NIDHI-PRAYAS (DST, up to ₹10 lakh, explicitly no-incorporation-required for individual innovators) looks promising on its face, but IIT Kanpur's own guidelines state flatly that "projects relating to software development... are not eligible," and Zone is a software-only Android app — this needs direct confirmation with a PRAYAS Centre on whether BLE-hardware-adjacent elements change that reading, but it should not be counted on ([IIT Kanpur NIDHI-PRAYAS Guidelines](https://iitk.ac.in/invent/NIDHI-PRAYAS-Guidelines.html)). Smart India Hackathon has no DPIIT or incorporation requirement at all, but SIH 2026's college-internal deadlines have already passed as of this writing (grand finale reportedly December 2026), making SIH 2027 — not this cycle — the real target; the team should ask SMIT's SIH SPOC directly whether any late-entry route exists for the current cycle regardless. CSR-funded student competitions (Viasat Space for Good India Challenge, Samsung Solve for Tomorrow) are mostly closed or mid-cycle for 2026, and no disaster-tech-specific CSR or UN/Red Cross humanitarian-innovation fund was confirmed as currently open and accessible to a team at this stage — a genuine evidentiary gap, not just a search miss, so these should be watched for 2027 cycles rather than planned around now. The one lead that stands out as purpose-built for exactly this team's profile is the **North East Entrepreneurship Development Programme (NEEDP)** — a ₹7.5 crore corpus run by IIM Calcutta Innovation Park (IIMCIP) and the North Eastern Council, explicitly designed to help student entrepreneurs across the eight NE states ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/iim-calcutta-innovation-park-north-east-entrepreneurship-start-up-assam-meghalaya-arunachal-pradesh)) — because IIMCIP has separately just been appointed **Sikkim's own state Lead Incubation Partner** under the "Sikkim Innovation Hub" / Sikkim INSPIRES programme, with district-level centres planned including one at Bardang ([Sikkim Industries Dept circular](https://industries.sikkim.gov.in/circulardoc/circulardoc-HBrVZ9CRo66PlXKpcvgoOqaHHTMcECe6NFsvsQHq.pdf)). That dual role — Northeast-wide fund administrator and Sikkim's own appointed partner — makes a direct email or visit to IIMCIP/the Sikkim Innovation Hub the single highest-value, lowest-effort next step available to the team right now, ahead of any further desk research. Separately, AIC-SMUTBI's own *incubation* program (distinct from its DPIIT-linked seed fund) explicitly allows unregistered teams to apply, requiring only that promoters incorporate within one year of incubation start — a meaningfully more permissive door than SISFS, and worth confirming directly ([AIC-SMUTBI Apply page](https://www.smutbi.com/apply/)).

## What to deliberately not chase yet

Two instruments are frequently mentioned alongside Zone's profile and should be named here only to be set aside correctly: the **Startup India Seed Fund Scheme (SISFS)** and **AIC-SMUTBI's own DPIIT-linked seed fund** both require DPIIT recognition plus incorporation no more than two years prior, Indian promoters holding ≥51% equity, and an eligible entity type (Pvt Ltd, registered partnership, or LLP) — an unregistered team simply cannot apply to either, and SISFS's officially stated operational window (April 2021–2025) was never confirmed as extended into 2026 in this research, so its live status needs checking at the official portal regardless of incorporation timing ([BankBazaar](https://www.bankbazaar.com/government-schemes/startup-india-seed-fund-scheme.html); [AIC-SMUTBI Apply page](https://www.smutbi.com/apply/)). The good news is that the gate itself is cheap and fast once the team decides to walk through it: a two-director Pvt Ltd company costs roughly **₹7,000–25,000 all-in** and takes **5–15 working days** once paperwork (DSC, DIN, PAN, name approval) is ready, with DPIIT recognition itself typically adding another 2–7 working days on top for a clean application — call it **three to four weeks and under ₹25,000** from "decide to incorporate" to "DPIIT-recognized and SISFS-eligible," figures that should be treated as estimates pending direct confirmation at mca.gov.in, since no official MCA fee schedule was consulted directly in this research pass. The strategic question is not cost or speed — both are small — it's *timing*: incorporating is the moment the team takes on ongoing compliance obligations (annual ROC filings, mandatory auditor appointment within 30 days, commencement-of-business filing within 180 days) while still students, so it should be triggered by having a field-piloted product and a credible institutional buyer conversation in hand, not by the availability of a seed fund alone.

## Conclusion

The evidence converges on a specific, almost boring, near-term plan: spend the next 6–12 months and roughly ₹1–1.5 lakh proving the hardware assumptions empirically (H0's capability probe is not a formality — it is the only reliable data source on Coded PHY and barometer presence that exists anywhere, public or private), field-validate on real Sikkim terrain, and use that evidence to have one real conversation with IIMCIP/the Sikkim Innovation Hub before chasing any other funding channel. The deeper insight from the competitive and revenue research is that Zone's silence-as-signal mechanism is a genuinely novel application of a known distributed-systems pattern to a problem — person-level survival inference via spontaneous, hand-to-hand phone relay — that no funded competitor, consumer app, or academic system has shipped, which gives the team a real differentiation story to tell a future institutional buyer; but every durable precedent in this category (Everbridge, goTenna, Bridgefy's pivot) says that story only converts to revenue through government or institutional licensing, years after a working pilot, not through a consumer app or a grant alone. Incorporation and DPIIT recognition are not obstacles to overcome quickly — they are a deliberate later step the team should trigger once they have piloted evidence to show a buyer, not before.
